Windmill Wiring Guide

Step-by-step wiring for the motorised windmill: first on a breadboard, one stage at a time in the order the firmware tasks need it, then as the permanent build, where the mill lifts off its electronics plate by unplugging two connectors. Tick steps off as you go; your ticks are kept in this browser only.

Part A

Breadboard bench build

Wire one stage at a time. Each stage unlocks a firmware task, so you can test what you just wired before adding the next part.

A1 · C3 and power rails

Firmware task 1

Ready when: the rails read about 5V. You can now do the first USB flash and see the windmill appear in Home Assistant.

A2 · Stepper driver and motor

Firmware task 2

Why GPIO2 stays empty. GPIO2 is a boot-strapping pin: Espressif's documentation says it must be high when the chip resets for a normal boot. A ULN2003 input would pull it towards ground through about 13 kΩ, so the stepper uses GPIO0, 1, 3 and 4 and skips it. If the C3 ever fails to boot, check that nothing is connected to GPIO2, GPIO8 or GPIO9.

Ready when: it boots five times out of five. Then turn on "Sails Turning" in Home Assistant: the four LEDs on the ULN2003 board chase and the motor shaft turns.

A3 · Pixel lead and pixels

Firmware task 5

Ready when: each Home Assistant light lights only its own pixel, and red, green and blue look right. Wrong colours usually mean the pixels use a different colour order, or they are RGBW parts: tell Claude.

A4 · Button

Firmware task 7

Ready when: a short press turns the whole mill on, and the next short press turns it off. While the sails turn, holding it for 1–5 s and letting go reverses them.

A5 · 470µF and the one-hour run

Firmware task 12

Ready when: the rail holds above 4.5V and an hour of sails with steady lights shows no flicker. Whether you fitted a pixel capacitor tells you whether the permanent build needs one.

Part B

Permanent build

There is no base box. The C3 and the ULN2003 sit on a small electronics plate fixed to the mounting pad, inside the mill's ground floor. The mill comes down over the plate and lifts straight off it. Two connectors split the two, the motor's own 5-pin JST-XH and a 4-pin JST: the mill side carries the motor and pixel wiring and gets glued into the tower; the plate side stays reachable whenever the mill is lifted. Only the USB cable leaves the mill, out through a notch at the back of the base ring and away behind scenery. Do every test in B5 before any part of the tower is closed.

Your C3 board's own connectors. The C3 board has three JSTs: a 2-pin for 5V/GND, the 5-way stepper header (GPIO0, 1, 2, 3, 4 with GPIO2's position empty, the middle one of five) and a 2-pin for data/button. Keep both 2-pin connectors inside the plate and never unplug them: if the 5V/GND lead went into the data/button socket, 5V would land on GPIO5 and GPIO6 and could kill the C3. Label them, or make one a 3-pin housing with a blank position so they cannot swap.

Where the diode, capacitor and resistor go. They sit on the mill side of the 4-pin plug, in the short pigtail that comes down the central passage into the ground floor. When the mill is lifted, the pigtail comes up with it and the parts are still reachable.

Spare conductor. If you might add a fifth pixel to light the sails later, use a 5-pin JST for the mill plug now and run a spare wire up the tower while it is open.

Troubleshooting

If something is wrong

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
C3 does not bootSomething on a boot-strapping pin (GPIO2, GPIO8, GPIO9)Check nothing is connected to those three pins.
Motor buzzes or shakes but does not turnIN1–IN4 out of order, or the ULN2003 power jumper is offCheck GPIO0, 1, 3 and 4 go to IN1–IN4 in order; refit the jumper.
C3 resets when the sails startSupply dips on the shared 5V railUse the 2A charger, not a laptop port. Fit the 470µF at the ULN2003.
No pixel lightsLead on the output end, diode backwards, or data not reaching the first pixelCheck the arrows, the diode stripe towards the pixels, and the 330R in the data wire.
Colours wrong (red shows green)Different colour order, or RGBW pixelsTell Claude; it is a one-line firmware change.
Pixels flicker only when the motor runsStepper noise on the data line or the supplyFit the 470µF and a pixel capacitor, twist data with GND, and move the data wire away from the coil wires.
Charger gives no power through a USB-C to USB-C cableThe board lacks the USB-C CC resistorsUse a USB-A to USB-C cable.

Wire colours in this guide are suggestions for clarity, except the motor's own blue, pink, yellow, orange and red, which are fixed. Voltages assume the 1N5408 diode drop for the pixels and USB-C power into the C3 (decided 2 October 2026). The electronics live on a plate inside the mill's ground floor with no base box and no ballast; the stepper uses GPIO0, 1, 3 and 4 with GPIO2 empty, pixel data is on GPIO6, the button is a doorbell beside the door, and the pixel capacitor is fitted only if the one-hour run needs it (decided 3 October 2026).
